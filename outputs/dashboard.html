Why this question

GDP decides aid allocations, debt sustainability assessments and domestic policy. In much of Sub-Saharan Africa it rests on thin statistical foundations — Jerven's point. Night lights, measured from orbit on a fixed instrument, are an independent check that no statistics office can revise.

What the data says so far

  • The two series disagree, consistently and in one direction. Lights outgrew reported GDP in 69% of country-years and in 32 of 38 countries. That is a pattern to explain, not a residual to ignore.
  • But the obvious explanations fail. Neither statistical capacity nor political accountability moves the gap once country and year effects are taken out. Both coefficients sit on zero.
  • And the gap's own premise fails too. GDP and lights do not move one-for-one: β̂ = 0.056, not 1. The gap is a difference between two growth rates measured on different things, not a structural elasticity.
  • Measurement is doing real work in the middle of all this. In 2017 a VIIRS compositing change lifted median lights growth to 0.183 against 0.075 in a typical year — and it hit dim countries hardest, so year effects cannot remove it.
  • GDP gets rewritten as you watch. 26 base-year changes across 24 of the 38 countries inside six years. Half the region restated its national accounts during the window.

What this is not

Divergence is not evidence of manipulation, and the dashboard is built so it cannot be read that way by accident.

  • Lights miss agriculture, the informal economy and anything off-grid — all large in this region.
  • Lights add things GDP does not: gas flaring, and new electricity connections that light a village without changing output much.
  • The satellite record has its own breaks, which is why every chart offers a bright-cells series alongside the headline one.

So what is it for

A country with a persistent, unexplained gap is a country worth looking at closely — with its statistics office's own releases, its electrification programme and its rebasing history in hand. The ranking chooses where to look. It does not settle anything.

On the null results: they are bounds, not absences. This design could only have detected a capacity effect larger than 0.32 of a within-country standard deviation and an accountability effect larger than 0.11 of a within-country standard deviation. Smaller real effects would have been invisible here, and saying "no relationship" would overclaim.

The models, and what every symbol means

Three equations. The first defines what is plotted; the second is what is estimated; the third tests whether the first one was a fair thing to plot.

1 · The outcome
gapit  =  Δln GDPit  −  Δln Lit

The outcome. How much faster reported GDP grew than night lights did, in country i in year t. Negative means lights grew faster.

On the charts Every bar in the gap chart, and the vertical distance between the two lines in the growth trace.

2 · Main specification
gapit = α + θ·SPIit + φ·Accit + π′Xit + μi + λt + εit

The main specification, estimated with country and year fixed effects and standard errors clustered by country.

Read θ on the absolute gap and φ on the signed gap — those are the two the design predicts a sign for.

3 · Check model
Δln GDPit = α + β·Δln Lit + γ·(Δln Lit×SPIit) + δ·(Δln Lit×Accit) + π′Xit + μi + λt + εit

The check model. The gap above is this model with β forced to 1 and γ = δ = 0, so estimating β tests the gap's central assumption instead of assuming it.

Result β̂ = 0.056, and H₀: β = 1 is rejected (t = -37.3). So the gap is a difference in two growth rates, not an elasticity.

Symbol by symbol

icountry

One of the 38 countries in the balanced panel.

One row in the ranking, one dot in the scatter.

tyear

2016 to 2021, six years.

The x-axis of every time-series chart.

Δlnlog difference

ln(value this year) − ln(value last year). A growth rate that is unit-free, so a country's own currency cancels out. 0.05 ≈ 5% growth.

Both lines in the growth trace.

Lnight lights

Total VIIRS radiance summed inside the country's borders. The bright-cells version counts only cells above a fixed 5 nW/cm²/sr floor.

The violet line; the rose dashed line is the bright-cells version.

θtheta — capacity effect

What the dissertation is testing. How much the gap moves with statistical capacity, within a country. Expected negative on the ABSOLUTE gap: better statistics, smaller disagreement either way.

The slope you would see in the scatter if capacity explained divergence. Estimated at -0.00001, not distinguishable from zero.

φphi — accountability effect

How much the gap moves with political accountability. Expected negative on the SIGNED gap: weaker accountability, GDP grows faster than lights.

The scatter's accountability axis. Estimated at -0.0048, not distinguishable from zero.

βbeta — lights elasticity

How much reported GDP moves when lights move 1%. The simple gap assumes this is exactly 1.

Not drawn directly. Estimated at 0.056, and H₀: β = 1 is rejected — so the gap is a difference in growth rates, not an elasticity.

γ, δgamma, delta — interactions

Whether the lights-to-GDP relationship itself depends on capacity or accountability, rather than just shifting the gap up or down.

Not drawn. Both are small and insignificant.

Xcontrols

Agriculture share, change in electricity access, urban share, log population, COVID stringency. Things that move the gap for mechanical reasons.

The scatter's electrification and agriculture axes show two of them.

μicountry fixed effect

One intercept per country. Absorbs each country's average gap and anything about it that does not change — geography, currency, how its statistics office works.

Why the prediction line sits near each country's own average rather than near zero.

λtyear fixed effect

One intercept per year. Absorbs shocks common to all countries, such as the global part of COVID. It CANNOT absorb the 2017 detection change, because that hit dim countries harder than bright ones.

Why the year distribution chart matters: 2017 is low for a measurement reason, not an economic one.

εitresidual

What the model does not explain for that country-year.

The vertical distance between a bar and the dashed prediction line.

Estimated with linearmodels.PanelOLS, entity and time effects, standard errors clustered by country (38 clusters, which is close to the rule-of-thumb minimum of about 30 and belongs in the limitations). Capacity and accountability are centred on their sample means, so the intercept and the interaction terms are read at average capacity and accountability rather than at zero, which is outside the data.
